Declamation Speech – Bhagat Singh
Brothers and Sisters of my beloved India,
Today I stand before you not as a rebel, not as a terrorist as the British call me, but as a son of Mother India who dreams of her freedom. We do not want mercy, we want justice. We do not beg for life, we demand liberty — for every farmer, every worker, every child born on this sacred soil. When I threw the bomb in the Assembly, I did not wish to kill anyone. I wanted the deaf to hear!


It was a cry against injustice, a call for awakening! Our aim was not destruction — it was revolution, Inquilab Zindabad!
Revolution does not mean bloodshed. Revolution means the birth of a new order — where oppression ends, where exploitation dies, and where freedom breathes in every heart. They may hang me. But remember, they can kill my body, not my ideas. The revolution is not of swords and guns — it is of minds and souls.


We must educate ourselves, we must awaken our youth, and we must fight — not with hatred, but with courage and conviction.
Let every Indian rise with pride and say: We will not live as slaves. We will live with dignity. And if we must die, we will die for our Motherland.
Inquilab Zindabad!!
